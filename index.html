About Our Project

Dataset & question

The dataset looks at how household income percentile relates to life expectancy among U.S. adults using data from The Association Between Income and Life Expectancy in the United States, 2001–2014. Table 1 combines data across multiple years to provide overall national estimates, while Table 2 breaks the estimates down by year, gender, and income percentiles. We focus on whether the life expectancy gap between higher and lower-income adults differs for men and women and how that gap changes over the years.

Visual design & interactions

To visualize our data, we decided to choose a line chart to show how a person's life expectancy can vary with different income percentiles. A second line chart then shows how life expectancy changes over time for the two selected income groups. These selections remain consistent in the dashboard, with matching colors to make them easy to follow. We also considered bar charts to represent the data, but a line chart better shows the trends over time than a bar chart.

Interactions Chosen:

  • Dynamic Filtering: Added dropdown menus for the user to choose gender, year, life expectancy measure, and income percentiles.
  • Linked Selection: Clicking the income gradient chart adjusts the comparison chart for the selected income percentile.
  • Details on Demand: Hovering over either chart displays exact numerical values.
  • Highlighting: Colored markers and guide lines show the selected income percentiles and year.

Interpretation & limitations

The charts that were created show the relationship between income and life expectancy, but they don't prove that income solely determines how long a person lives. The estimates only apply to people who have reached age 40 and can vary every year. The dropdown lets users compare race-adjusted estimates, which account for differences in racial composition across income groups, with unadjusted estimates. Additionally, the data covers 2001–2014, so it cannot be used to represent life expectancy today because factors affecting life expectancy may have changed since then.

Development process

We collaborated on most areas of Assignment 1 and discussed decisions to ensure we agreed on the direction of the project. Aryaman selected the dataset and variables we wanted to analyze. He also guided the initial design using techniques from our previous labs, including D3 scales, axes, and dropdown filtering. Aryaman developed much of the site and its two charts himself, using Codex assistance when needed, especially to debug the highlighting interactions and connections between the two datasets. We reviewed the resulting code and checked that the charts reflected the selected variables correctly. Aryaman also wrote the dataset introduction and published the GitHub Pages site. Aarav used Codex to help refine the code for readability and improve the dashboard styling. He also wrote most of the write-up, including the design rationale, development process, and AI usage sections, and recorded a live demonstration. Code development, debugging, and review took approximately 4 hours, while the write-up took approximately another 2 hours. Code development took the most time because it involved implementing our design decisions and debugging the interactions between the charts. Overall, we spent approximately 7–8 hours creating an interactive dashboard that explores income and life expectancy.

AI usage

The main AI tool we used was Codex, which helped fill the gaps between what we learned in class and what we wanted to implement for this project. For example, after deciding to highlight the gap between selected income percentiles in the first graph, we used Codex to help update the highlighted area when users changed their selections. Similarly, Codex helped debug the connections between the two tables so that the charts displayed the correct information for the selected gender, year, and income percentiles, and helped make the dashboard more visually appealing with colors and useful guide lines that made the selections easier to follow. Most of the text was written by us, but ChatGPT helped with phrasing, grammatical corrections, and explaining our comparisons more clearly. We reviewed the resulting code and checked the charts and interactions for errors. This project showed us how we could combine what we learned in class with our own design choices and AI assistance to develop an interactive visualization.
